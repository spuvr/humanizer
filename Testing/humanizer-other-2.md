Exceptions let you pull error handling out of the main flow of your code. Instead of every function returning some `(result, error)` thing the caller has to check, you throw when something goes wrong and a `catch` further up the stack deals with it. The catch (pun intended) is that control flow gets less obvious, exceptions travel further than you'd expect, and a lot of "graceful handling" in the wild is really just a `catch (Exception e) {}` that quietly eats the bug. They're useful. They're also easy to misuse.
